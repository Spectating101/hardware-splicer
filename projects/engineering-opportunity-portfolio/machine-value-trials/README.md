# Where Hardware Splicer could pay for its engineering time

Taiwan workshop screen, 19 November 2026. A family of switch testers is worth trying digitally for repeated tests on higher-value assemblies. Buy commodity replacement switches for routine repairs. The strongest industrial evidence for paying a designer is product-specific robot tooling. A coating machine offers a larger possible purchase saving, conditional on the lab actually needing repeatable coating.

## A tester for salvaged switches and refurbished controls

A technician puts a sample in a holder. A servo presses and releases it. The controller checks electrical contact in both positions and logs a failure. A new holder lets the same station test a different switch shape. This makes sense for characterizing samples or investigating an intermittent failure. For occasional continuity checks, use an existing meter. For repeated tests, count only operator time freed after loading, setup, observation and unloading.

For a single switch and one holder, budget NT$800 in materials plus four hours at NT$600, or NT$3,200 (US$97) with an existing computer and printing access. Hunan Gonoava lists a US$300 sample price for its GA-3405. ### What a press and a test job cost

The published tester monitors the two switch terminals as a servo pushes the plunger. On closure it waits 100 ms, then releases the plunger until the circuit opens. The controller reports the commanded servo positions over USB. Reaching a travel limit without the electrical transition flags a missed press or a stuck switch. The builder estimated 0.94 seconds per complete cycle on one setup. At that rate, 20 cycles take about 19 seconds, 300 take 4 minutes 42 seconds and 10,000 take 2 hours 37 minutes, before handling or interruptions. During the original overnight run the USB logger stopped after a controller reset. The builder estimated that around 27,000 presses went unrecorded. A log gap makes that interval inconclusive. [Builder's measurements and video](https://incoherency.co.uk/blog/stories/keyswitch-tester.html)

A Taiwan retailer lists ten 6 × 6 × 9 mm tactile switches for NT$27. At NT$600 per labor hour, one new NT$2.70 switch buys only 16 seconds of handling. Even one minute spent salvaging and mounting a loose switch costs NT$10 before running the test. A better candidate is a repaired control panel whose installed button sometimes misses a command. That requires its own holder and a way to read the panel's actual response. [Jin Hua ten-pack listing](https://jin-hua.com.tw/page/product/show.aspx?lang=TW&num=2169)

For that panel, suppose a manual repeated-press check takes six minutes of operator attention and the fixture takes one minute to load, unload and review. Each panel frees five minutes worth NT$50, so 64 panels cover the estimated NT$3,200 station. If attention falls by only one minute, it takes 320 panels. Add any panel-specific design and integration cost to the numerator. At this labor rate, an extra two hours raises those thresholds to 88 and 440 panels respectively. These are break-even scenarios with no measured shop volume or physical validation.

## Robot gripper adapters and alignment fixtures

A refurbishing cell could use one purchased gripper with changeable fingers and a locating tray for each family of housings. One variant could grip a small router enclosure while keeping its power jack accessible. Another could locate a handheld controller beneath a test probe. A stock parallel gripper or a hand-operated nest is the baseline whenever it already holds the part reliably.

In CNC Würfel's industrial gripper-adapter case, HP reports €1,500–2,000 for design and €200 for printing. Design represented 88–91% of those two costs. This is a concrete example of the cost HS is meant to reduce. A second factory example comes from Volkswagen Autoeuropa, which reported €400 outsourced and €10 per part in-house for a liftgate badge placement tool. The reports describe factory-specific equipment, accounting and volumes. No local outsourced quote or HS design-time saving has been measured for either example. ## A programmable dip coater for material samples

This machine lowers a test piece into a bath, holds it and withdraws it at a chosen speed. A lab comparing protective coatings could reuse the drive and design different sample holders. A published enclosed machine had a US$569 hardware bill in 2022. A separate study reports approximately US$50 of materials for a smaller machine tested on 25 × 25 mm glass samples. A third group demonstrated a recycled printer stepper on a portable coater. These are three different designs. Salvaged hardware has no savings allowance in the calculation. [Enclosed machine and BOM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9526236/) · [Small-sample machine](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4052/6/4/75) · [Printer motor reuse](https://quimicanova.sbq.org.br/Content/pdf/v45n1a12.pdf)

Using the US$569 BOM as a starting estimate, add NT$2,000 for substitutions and NT$7,200 for twelve hours of sourcing, design, assembly and checking. Total **NT$27,947 / US$848**. The Ni-Lo X2SL lists for **NT$49,388 / US$1,499** before freight. The modeled gap is **NT$21,441 / US$651**. A sourcing directory also advertises a generic vertical machine from US$800. A comparable unit delivered for less than NT$27,947 would beat this build estimate. Obtain the actual travel, withdrawal speed and repeatability needed and a delivered quote before assigning a purchase saving. For a few occasional samples, shared equipment or hand dipping may cover the job. HS has not run or validated either coater design. ## An automatic pickup coil winder

A purchased Lortec auto-traverse winder lists for US$249, about NT$8,204. Give DIY a US$120 materials budget and six engineering and assembly hours and it already costs **NT$7,554 / US$229**. The remaining NT$650 buys about 65 minutes to resolve any extra work. The US$120 figure is a scenario assumption. A different builder estimates under US$100 excluding filament for an early prototype. For standard pickups the purchased unit is the stronger default. A custom bobbin would justify revisiting that decision only if an adapter on the purchased winder cannot handle it economically. [Buy&Ship listing](https://www.buyandship.today/en/shopping/marketplaces/ebay/products/v1%7C800486922576%7C0/) · [Separate DIY prototype](https://pickupforge.com/pickupforge-w1.html)

HS read the public controller project and two mechanical meshes, exported a 16-component, 42-net schematic netlist, and identified three dangling track ends among the design-rule findings. Those flags require review. HS also generated a single-solid STEP carrier around the bare controller board. It has no mounting holes and omits component, connector and retention clearance. This demonstrates a useful pre-order inspection and a CAD starting point. It provides no measurement of winding performance.
