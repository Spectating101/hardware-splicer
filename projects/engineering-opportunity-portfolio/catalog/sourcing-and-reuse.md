# What Hardware Splicer can reuse

The value of HS in this portfolio is the ability to change the part that makes a machine specific to its job. A standard arm, purchased camera or existing printer can provide motion or sensing. The shop can design only the holder, end effector, fitting or acceptance fixture that the buyer needs. The returns model charges for engineering every time a new design is launched.

## Two decisions before spending on automation

**Price the donor's time.** For a recovered part, expected cost per usable part = (donor purchase + collection + teardown labor + test labor + disposal) / expected good parts, less genuine recoverable by-product proceeds per good part. Example: ten candidate parts, NT$300 acquisition and 45 minutes of work at NT$600/hour, with six good parts, cost **NT$125 each** before disposal. A new equivalent at NT$100 is already cheaper. With the same six good parts and NT$300 acquisition, only 30 minutes of total labor fit a NT$100 replacement budget. A confirmed parts list and yield can be entered in the workbook for each affected product.

**Price the automated operation per accepted unit.** Suppose an NT$7,088 arm and ten engineering hours cost NT$13,088 before fixtures. If each accepted unit eliminates a net eight minutes of operator time, the saving is NT$80 per unit at NT$600/hour. **164 accepted units** repay that investment before maintenance, fixture costs and downtime. A 25-unit run saves only NT$2,000 on those assumptions. The same cell serving several repeat products may reach the threshold if their actual demand and changeover times support it.
