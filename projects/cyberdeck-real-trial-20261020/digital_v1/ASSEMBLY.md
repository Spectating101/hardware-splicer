# Fielddeck 7 assembly and service plan

This documents an eventual prototype assembly matching the delivered solids. The present work stops at a digital prototype. No parts have been bought, printed, assembled or powered.

## Coordinate and fastener reference

All dimensions are millimetres. X runs left to right, Y from the keyboard/front toward the display/rear, Z up from the case floor. Main case: 310 × 285 × 64; feet add 6 mm below, fan guard adds 2 mm above. Main tray/face seams are X=155 and Y=140 with a 0.30 mm gap. Each STL is shifted to a local print origin; the STEP assembly retains world placement.

`cad/geometry_report.json` contains every display, Pi, lid and seam fastener centre. Four display case mounts are at X=85/225, Y=174.645/245.355, with a seat at Z=52.54. These are the display's outer case lugs, not its separate Pi mounting posts. Pi mounting is the manufacturer 58 × 49 mm pattern, world centres (56.5,201.5), (56.5,259.5), (7.5,201.5), (7.5,259.5), PCB bottom Z=7. Keyboard supports end at Z=38; eight compressed 2 mm pad pairs give the intended underside Z=40. Bank envelope occupies X=16–68.3, Y=25–129, Z=4–30.

## Print preparation

Print the **20** listed custom parts: four trays, four face pieces, six seam plates, fan duct, fan guard and four feet. All fit a nominal 220 × 220 mm bed individually; the largest footprint is approximately 155 × 145 mm. Millimetres must be selected when importing STL. Do not print purchased module models.

Use PETG, 0.2 mm layers, four perimeters, eight top/bottom layers and 30% infill as starting settings. Tray floors, seam plates and feet lie flat. Faces print underside down; the shallow screen recess needs short bridges under the retaining lip. The display posts bridge a 6 mm underside tool shaft. Face-boss nuts enter sideways through 2.6 mm-high slots beneath 1.8 mm roofs; those roofs carry the face retention load. Review those bridges and nut-pocket roofs in the slicer; supports and settings are not validated. Exported solid volume gives a **100% solid mass upper bound near 840 g**. Actual sliced mass, supports and print time have not been calculated; do not multiply by 30% to estimate filament use.

Before a complete print, the eventual operator should make a small hole/nut coupon with 2.8/3.2 mm clearance holes, 5.2/5.8 mm hex pockets, and the 2.6 mm side-entry slot with its 1.8 mm roof. Adjust for the actual printer. Allowance values are explicit design choices, not calibrated fit tolerances. Files are authored in `author_cad.py`; coordinate changes need rerunning the complete geometry checks, not merely stretching an STL.

## Assembly order

1. Deburr/inspect printed pieces. Join the four tray quarters using six internal seam plates, **24 M3×10 screws and 24 M3 nuts**. Screws enter from below the 3 mm floor, nuts above the 3 mm plates. Seat the four adhesive feet to keep heads clear of the bench. The seams remain serviceable; no glue is required to close them.
2. Place four M2.5 nuts in underside Pi pockets. Install the prepared microSD in the Pi. Seat the Pi on its 4 mm printed standoffs, connectors toward the rear opening; use **four M2.5×10** screws from above. The board must sit on its four seats, with no force on connectors. The wide rear opening intentionally also leaves cable-return space next to USB/Ethernet.
3. Thread the two 20 mm bank straps through their four floor slots. Cut the 1 mm EPDM pad to 52×104 mm and place below the bank to match its Z=4 seat; confirm compressed thickness. Seat the unchanged commercial bank in the nest, leave buttons and plugs unobstructed, and retain it. No pressure against its display or integrated cable. Confirm physical orientation/plug reach before securing other modules.
4. Place the Touch Display 2 on the four outer-lug supports, glass toward Z=62. Use **four M2.5×6** screws upward through the underside tool shafts. The nominal 3 mm seats leave about 3 mm engagement; confirm the actual lug thread and safe depth. These screws must not bottom into the panel. Keep the display's own four Pi posts unused. Tighten only enough to seat it; the bezel is not a glass clamp.
5. Connect the module cables using `CONNECTIONS.md`. Insulate and retain both display power extension joins. Keep wire loops away from the posts, seams, fan blades and screw tips. Do not crease the FFC. The keyboard/fan USB plugs use rear external service loops returning through the open portion beside the ports.
6. While the faces are still off, slide **12 M3 nuts** sideways into the boss slots. Each nut seats against the underside of a **1.8 mm printed roof**, with the nut top at Z=59.2. The roof must remain intact; an open-top nut pocket would allow the entire bolted face and nut to lift free of the tray. The nut-entry directions point away from adjacent bosses/walls and are checked in the exported geometry.
7. Preassemble the fan on the **loose rear-left face outside the case**. Orient it to blow down toward the Pi; place the guard above the face and the fan below. Slide the duct sleeve around the bottom of the frame, with its **2 mm flange beneath the fan**, then pass **four M3×25 screws** downward through guard, face, fan and duct flange. Tighten **four M3 nuts below the flange** using an 8 mm maximum outer-diameter socket. The clamp stack is 17 mm plus 2.4 mm nut; nominal screw tips extend 5.6 mm below the nuts. The duct is retained by bolts, with no adhesive load path. Connect/route the supplied fan USB adapter clear of the rotor before lowering this face into position. A 40 mm fan and 44 mm duct cannot be installed through the 36 mm inlet after the face is fitted.
8. Install the four face pieces, including the fan/duct subassembly, with **12 M3×10** screws into the captured nuts. The screen pocket leaves nominal 0.3 mm vertical clearance under the bezel and 0.6 mm radial clearance around the glass. The opening covers only the inactive border in the vendor reference geometry. There must be no face pressure on the active glass. Right-side slots provide exhaust; geometry does not establish actual airflow or CPU temperature.
9. Apply eight 10×10 mm hook-loop pad pairs above the keyboard columns. Confirm **2 mm compressed thickness** or adjust/shim the supports. Place the keyboard in its 287×125 mm opening, tail through the centre notch. No edge clamp overlaps the keys. Lift it from the rear service gap to reach the bank; unplug/allow slack in its cable before lifting.

## Fastener and tool envelopes

The selected cap-head dimensions are [M3: 5.5 mm head diameter × 3 mm height](https://www.accu.co.uk/metric-cap-head-screws/3819-SSCF-M3-10-A2) and [M2.5: 4.5 × 2.5 mm](https://www.accu.co.uk/metric-cap-head-screws/3806-SSCF-M2-5-10-A2). The selected DIN 934 nuts are [M3: 5.5 mm AF × 2.4 mm high](https://www.accu.co.uk/hexagon-nuts/7888-HPN-M3-A2) and [M2.5: 5 mm AF × 2 mm high](https://www.accu.co.uk/hexagon-nuts/7886-HPN-M2-5-A2). The exported-part verifier checks heads, nuts, screw shafts/tips, bearing surfaces and insertion paths. Driver shafts must fit a **5.6 mm diameter** envelope; the long display access needs a straight reach of approximately 50 mm. Seam/fan nut sockets must have **8 mm maximum outer diameter**. Full handles/finger access and tightening torque have not been modelled. Assemble the seams and fan subassembly while both sides are accessible.

## Service and unresolved fits

Shut down Linux and disconnect the bank before opening. The keyboard is removable for bank access; full display/Pi access uses the bolted face pieces. Unplug the fan USB lead before lifting the rear-left face; the fan and bolted duct lift with that face. The bank recharges outside the device. There is no sealed enclosure, waterproofing, drop rating, hinge, built-in charger/UPS or exposed GPIO instrumentation port.

The display uses an approximate manufacturer STEP with its own fit disclaimer. Keyboard, bank, Pi connectors and fan use nominal envelope models; plug positions, keyboard underside curvature/feet, compressed pad thickness and ribbon bends need eventual measurement. The current digital solid check establishes geometric consistency for those models. It cannot establish a first-print fit or a working powered device.
