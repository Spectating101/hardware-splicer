## Eligibility conclusion

Hardware-Splicer should **not** be entered as an undergraduate Tertiary Student Project merely because the developer is a student.

The 2026 category framework defines Tertiary Student Project as undergraduate work.

The Taiwan 2026 selection guideline separately makes public/private university **master's and doctoral students** eligible under the **Research & Development** participation route, with:

- team size no more than five;
- one adviser required;
- valid student ID or proof of enrollment.

This resolves the master's-level eligibility concern.

## Mandatory-category structure

The 2026 guideline states that every submitted product/solution must select a main category before the organizer can confirm optional Cross Category and Technology entries.

The undergraduate Student category does not fit a master's entrant. The strongest working main-category choice for Hardware-Splicer is therefore:

> **Industrial (HC-I)**

because the official Industrial scope covers automation through integration of systems, technologies and processes in sectors including manufacturing, and Hardware-Splicer's bounded value proposition is semiconductor test/validation support-hardware readiness.

### Category stack to pursue

**Working recommendation — confirm with Taiwan secretariat during registration:**

1. **HC-I Industrial** — main category;
2. **CC-RD Research & Development Project of the Year** — optional cross category;
3. **CT-AI Artificial Intelligence Technology of the Year** — optional technology category.

The organizer explicitly retains authority to review and adjust competition categories. If the registration UI or secretariat directs postgraduate R&D entrants to another mandatory head category, preserve the project claims and change only the overlay/category selection.

### Fallback main category

**Business Services (HC-BS)** is the plausible fallback if the secretariat considers Hardware-Splicer primarily a cross-industry professional/ICT engineering service rather than an industrial/manufacturing solution.

Do not choose the fallback merely because it sounds more software-like; the product's actual bounded domain is industrial hardware readiness.

## Why Industrial fits the current evidence

Official Industrial weighting:

- Uniqueness — 30%
- Market Potential — 20%
- Functionalities and Features — 30%
- Quality & Application of Technology — 20%

Hardware-Splicer's strongest current material maps to uniqueness and functionality:

- source-blind embedded engineer + outer evaluator;
- unresolved identity preservation;
- deterministic evidence/authority boundaries;
- revision invalidation;
- physical-evidence architecture;
- concrete fail-closed simulation-status correction.

The weak area is still market/pilot evidence, not architecture.

## Why R&D is a strong optional category

The official R&D category explicitly includes postgraduate tertiary student research and is for innovative ICT products/processes/services that can demonstrate functionality while still incomplete/not yet marketed.

Official weighting:

- Uniqueness — 30%
- Proof of Concept — 30%
- Functionalities and Features — 20%
- Quality & Application of Technology — 20%

This maps unusually well to Hardware-Splicer if the proof tranche produces:

- genuine live model evidence;
- fresh unseen-case evidence;
- one real revision-bound physical case.

The R&D rubric's Proof of Concept attributes explicitly include commercial potential and pilot trial, so the physical/pilot case is a high-value evidence multiplier.

## Why AI is a useful optional category

The official AI Technology category evaluates:

- Security — 30%
- Creativity — 30%
- Scale — 20%
- Openness / Integrability — 20%

The Hardware-Splicer narrative for this category should emphasize architecture and process safety rather than generic model intelligence:

- source-blind model operation;
- constrained tool/evidence boundaries;
- separation of model reasoning from physical authority;
- integration with deterministic engineering tools;
- vendor-neutral provider/runtime posture;
- third-party engineering workflow integration.

Do not claim compliance with external security standards unless evidence exists.

## Taiwan selection process

The official 2026 Taiwan selection guideline states:

- application through 2026-08-27;
- qualification review and second-stage review in September;
- Taiwan delegation announcement in October;
- training in October–November;
- international competition planned for December in Jakarta.

The second-stage presentation is in English and uses a 25-minute slot:

- 3 minutes equipment setup;
- 10 minutes product presentation/demo;
- 10 minutes Q&A;
- 2 minutes teardown.

This validates the canonical `DEMO_10_MINUTES.md` + `JUDGE_QA.md` packaging structure.
