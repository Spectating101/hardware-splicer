# Can Hardware Splicer run the engineering side of a reuse company?

**The business depends on making engineering iterations cheap enough for small orders.** A customer needs a machine to do a job. HS should make it affordable to design that machine with purchasable parts, revise it when requirements change and qualify donor substitutions when they make economic sense. A refurbisher's historical lots can help test the sourcing side. The cost of reaching an accepted design remains unmeasured.

## What an order can afford to spend on design

The selected starting model is to **build and publish our own product families**.
HS chooses the brief and owns the reusable design and acceptance checks. The
[PCB inspection nest](../projects/pcb-inspection-nest/README.md) is the first
digital release candidate in this experiment. The customer-service and
partner-lot scenarios below are earlier business hypotheses. For a self-published
design, count original family development, incremental variants and ongoing
support against actual demand as it becomes known.

Track the cost of the first accepted design and the additional cost of each accepted revision. Include paid or founder time spent specifying, sourcing engineering evidence, preparing inputs, operating tools, reviewing and correcting the result. Add model and compute costs across all attempts, including abandoned approaches. Record elapsed time separately. Keep shared HS development visible as a platform investment, and charge work unique to a customer's design to that job.

For a digital benchmark, give an HS-assisted engineer and a comparison engineer the same starting brief, source files and acceptance criteria. Record the effort to produce the first design. Then reveal one previously unseen change, such as a wider board for the inspection fixture. Each route must show that the camera still resolves the required detail across the larger field of view. Record what evidence carried over, what had to be checked again and how much reviewer time it took to accept the change. Include correction costs if a later review reopens it. A failed or unresolved design retains its spend in the results.

The affordable design budget is the expected order contribution after recurring costs, less the amount the company needs to retain for overhead and profit. In the six-sale illustration below, only $20 remains before design, overhead and taxes. Spending $20 on engineering alone consumes the entire surplus. For a donor substitution, compare its incremental engineering cost with the total savings on the units that donor can actually cover.

## From intake to a customer or recycler

| Shop step and accountable party | What HS can use today | Gap to close and evidence that would close it |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Receive a lot.** Supplier or shop records who transferred what, when, how many and under what terms. | Donor capture, photo/marking intake, listing JSON and declared inventory. | Import a partner manifest, retain source row IDs and photos, deduplicate retries, reconcile received counts and assign lot/unit IDs. Record ownership, location, data-bearing media and any quarantine. One pallet photo rarely establishes the count or exact revisions. |
| **2. Identify and grade each unit.** Technician establishes identity, completeness and condition. | Vision and teardown search suggest identities. The repair operator has blocked a donor whose architecture it could not establish. | Nearby board revisions can share names or housings. Record competing identities, label crops and source rights. Bind each test result to its unit, date, method and reviewer. A model trained on a known-good reference still needs the incoming unit's condition. |
| **3. Choose a route.** Planner compares whole-unit resale, repair, parts resale, conversion and downstream recycling. | Portfolio planner proposes builds, reusable blocks, missing capabilities and work orders. | Compare routes against the customer job, time, cost and available stock. Preserve the value of the intact donor. Rank the next inspection by whether its result could change the route. Include a hold or decline outcome. |
| **4. Source missing items and establish demand.** Buyer and sales operator gather different evidence. | File-based offer intake, BOM, stock matching and a pricing integration with fallback estimates. | Keep purchase asks, distributor quotes, sold comparables and customer orders distinct. Bind currency, region, condition, quantity, timestamps, freight and expiry to each. Refresh withdrawn offers. Label estimates. A supplier quote supplies no evidence of customer demand. |
| **5. Engineer the conversion.** Agent proposes the design and HS runs checks. | Substitution checks, source revisions, selected CAD/simulation jobs and package export. The virtual Ender-3 plotter includes a custom clamp and a replayed drawing path. | For a repeat SKU, specify allowable donor revisions, mounts, wiring, firmware and acceptance limits. Re-run affected checks after each substitution. Include test fixtures and time to assemble. Unknown interfaces and heterogeneous lots can erase reuse savings. |
| **6. Reserve and build.** Inventory system allocates each usable serial or lot. Technician records what goes into each output. | HS reads InvenTree and accounts for stock already allocated. Different portfolio proposals can reference the same item. | Give one system responsibility for reservations and consumption. Support concurrent orders, partial receipts, substitutions, cancellation and retries. At disassembly, retire the parent as sellable and create traceable child stock. Reconcile rejected material and process losses. |
| **7. Release, sell and support.** Operator verifies the actual unit, releases its listing and fulfils the order. | HS can produce a digital dossier, BOM, test plan and draft selling plan. | Bind inspection, functional acceptance and condition disclosure to the shipped serial. Define warranty, return route, replacement stock and traceability for affected buyers. Validate a marketplace seller account and fulfillment flow before publishing externally. |
| **8. Close the loop.** Operator records the actual outcome and the agent updates its forecasts. | HS retains engineering evidence and revisions. | Import realized sale price, time-to-sale, paid labor, yield per donor family, returns and downstream receipts. Track mass received, reused, recycled, held and discarded separately. Distinguish disposals from recovered material and record the basis for any environmental claim. |

Existing routes and a documented open case are available in the [salvage workflow](../apps/circuit-ai/docs/SALVAGE_TO_PRODUCT_WORKFLOW.md), [workshop operator receipts](../examples/workshop_operator/README.md) and [virtual plotter](../projects/ender3-penplotter-digital/README.md).

## How an agent would live inside HS

A proposed backend worker wakes up when a manifest arrives, a listing changes, a measurement is recorded or a customer requests a quantity. Its durable record is a work order containing the lot and unit IDs, customer requirements, evidence, current design revision, alternatives and outstanding decisions. On restart it resumes from that record. The existing MCP interface and engineering operator offer a starting point for invoking HS checks. [Current operator contract](../examples/workshop_operator/README.md)

| Example event | Agent action | What controls the outcome |
|---|---|---|
| A partner uploads a bin manifest and photos | Normalize rows, identify likely donor families, preserve uncertain matches, compare whole-unit and salvage options. | Source IDs and file hashes make repeated imports idempotent. Seller descriptions and uploaded documents are data. Only authenticated shop events change custody or test status. |
| A buyer asks for three inspection fixtures | Read free stock, estimate tested yield, generate alternatives, simulate supported designs, give a cost range and identify the bottleneck. | The inventory service controls quantities. Engineering checks consume versioned inputs. With one available camera, three units remain a sourcing proposal. |
| A controller offer disappears or a donor revision changes | Expire the quote, find alternatives, rerun affected checks and flag stale proposals and draft listings. | A revisioned evidence log records the change. Restarting or replaying the event produces the same state. |
| The webcam's mounting pattern is unknown | Request one dimensioned drawing or a measurement tied to that webcam. Show which decision depends on it. | A supplied measurement can unlock fit. Estimated geometry can support provisional CAD. A human or authorized test system supplies physical observations. |
| An opportunity appears profitable | Prepare a purchase proposal, draft outbound description and list the conditions behind each claim. | Separate service credentials for read access and transactions. An operator owns spend, external publication and unit release. Keep supplier/customer data scoped to their organization and log every consequential action. |

The useful memory is the history of each donor family, including failed conversions and returns. An agent can learn that a certain scanner usually costs more to dismantle than its carriage recovers. Prediction quality then improves from the shop's own outcomes. Feeding historical decisions back as if they were successful outcomes would reinforce mistakes.

## Where a business could make money

**First customer hypothesis:** a refurbisher or repair shop with a recurring stream of similar donors and a backlog of triage. The first offer could be a per-lot analysis service. Its value is technician time saved and better recovery compared with the shop's usual route. A second offer is tested replacement modules or conversion kits for a documented donor family. A finished fixture is a later option once buyers value a repeatable result and the shop can support variation and returns. Framework's commercial case for reusing a Laptop 13 mainboard shows the product form is real. Demand for a particular HS product still needs its own evidence. [Framework mainboard case](https://frame.work/product_contents/cooler-master-mainboard-case)

**Illustrative break-even in USD.** Suppose a ten-donor lot costs $40 delivered, intake uses two hours at $24/hour and residual handling costs $12. That is $100 committed to the lot. Suppose each unit built to order sells for $50 and costs $8 for added parts, $12 for assembly and test, $8 for fees and outbound fulfilment, and $2 for expected returns. Each sold unit contributes $20 toward the lot. Five sales break even, six leave $20, and four lose $20, before fixed overhead and taxes. If an intact-sale route would net $30 on the same lot after costs, at least seven conversions must sell to beat it. One additional hour of intake turns the six-sale case into a $4 loss. These chosen numbers illustrate a decision and are independent of the legacy model's $14.90 estimate. If units are built ahead of orders, also charge parts and labor for unsold units.

## A digital pilot with an explicit decision at the end

1. **Price the engineering work and verify the inventory inputs.** Run the first-design and changed-requirement benchmark above. For the sourcing side, import the same offer twice, issue a price update, expire it, then simulate a receipt of one unit. Expected counts are zero owned before receipt and one after it. Record disassembly and verify the intact donor cannot also be sold. Two competing plans must be unable to reserve the same last unit. Replay out-of-order events and restart midway.
2. **Replay historical lots.** Obtain a partner's permitted manifest and actual outcomes for several lots across at least two donor families. As a proposed starting sample, use 30 donor units. Hold back a whole later lot. Feed HS only information available on the decision date. Compare its route, unresolved cases, minutes per unit and net recovery with the shop's recorded baseline. A synthetic run tests software behaviour. Observed outcomes test the business assumption.
3. **Replay one buyer job.** Use one defined fixture or tested module family, a quantity and an acceptance condition. Model two donor revisions, a failed unit and a vanished offer. Deliver the allocated BOM, missing inputs, design files, evidence, ETA assumptions, draft listing and low/base/high economics. A single low-cost reference donor cannot establish repeatable supply.
4. **Decide whether a partner pilot merits investment.** Require zero phantom or double-allocated stock in the replay, every customer-facing technical claim traceable to evidence, and results that preserve uncertainty. Look for a recurring class where observed net recovery per technician hour beats intact resale or the shop's current route after failed units and unsold stock. Check whether a real operator would pay for that improvement. If only triage saves money, sell the analysis service. If buyers also want repeat units, pursue the kit. Physical yield, acceptance, warranty and actual willingness to pay remain open until a partner supplies that evidence.

Reproduce the two initial observations from the repository root with `.venv/bin/python examples/salvage_company/replay.py`. The run uses the existing backend with temporary local storage.
